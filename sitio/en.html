From space to the chakra.

Willak.space is a decision-support tool for agroecologists and field technicians who work with Kichwa families in Napo, in the Ecuadorian Amazon. It combines NASA open data, field measurements and what the family knows to decide what to plant, when, and how to rotate.

A chakra is a Kichwa agroforestry plot. It rotates through three stages: clearing with short-cycle crops, crops under shade (plantain, cacao, guayusa), and fallow (purma), when the forest grows back and the soil recovers.

What it does for each plot

  1. Downloads NASA and soil data for the exact location.
  2. Computes five traffic-light risks: erosion, nutrient leaching, acidity, extreme heat and dry spells. Each one shows its source, date and confidence level.
  3. Reads 25 years of greenness (EVI), discards cloudy dates and detects the stage of the chakra.
  4. Adds what the family says: priorities, planting history and local signs. When it disagrees with the satellite, the family wins.
  5. Recommends what to plant with a 0–100 score and an explanation.
  6. Builds a 3–5 year rotation plan that keeps the soil covered during the heavy-rain months.
  7. Monitors the crop and raises an alert, with a probable cause, when it grows less than expected.

Registering a plot takes one tap on the map. The field sheet, the plan and the family form work offline. The interface is in Spanish, the language of its users.

Actual status of each dataset. NASA POWER and GIBS run live from the browser. MODIS, IMERG and SRTM require an Earthdata account, which the project's server holds. That path is implemented and tested against a simulator of the services, but not yet with a real account. In this public demo, without the account, the app uses the backups (NASA POWER rainfall and a 90 m elevation model) and shows greenness only on the test plot, with clearly labelled illustrative data.

Other sources: SoilGrids 2.0 (ISRIC) for soil, Copernicus DEM via Open-Meteo, Esri World Imagery and OpenStreetMap.

How the data is processed

Method.

  • Clouds. Only pixels with pixel reliability 0 or 1 and no adjacent cloud or shadow are used. Terra and Aqua are merged. A date with less than 50 % valid area is marked missing.
  • Gaps. A Whittaker smoother with an upper envelope, because clouds lower EVI and do not raise it. Only gaps of up to 48 days are filled, and filled points are drawn differently from measured ones.
  • Chakra stage. Rules on annual EVI. For example, a clearing is a drop of 0.15 or more against the previous three years.
  • Recovery. Plot EVI against a nearby forest with stable greenness. At 85 % or more, the fallow is ready to be opened again.
  • Recommendation. A weighted average of rotation, soil, climate and family priorities. The user moves the weights and the ranking updates at once.
  • Alerts. Raised only on measured data, when EVI falls 15 % or more below the expected curve. The diagnosis rules out rainfall and heat before pointing to tired soil.

All thresholds and weights live in one configuration file. They are initial values, to be validated with the local research centre. The test plot uses illustrative data: it checks the logic; it is not a measurement.

Transparency

Use of artificial intelligence.

  • Images and video. The plants and the two woolly-monkey mascots were generated with Google Flow. They carry an "IA" (AI) mark on the site. The globe is NASA's Blue Marble image.
  • Code. Written and tested with the help of Claude (Anthropic), reviewed by the team.
  • What is not AI. The traffic light, the recommendation, the plan and the alerts are explicit rules and formulas, not an AI model.
